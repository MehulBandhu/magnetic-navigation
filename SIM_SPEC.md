# Magnetic navigation flight simulator: build specification

## What it is, in one paragraph

Three layers. The world: a real magnetic map (the Gawler survey grid, 45 m pixels), continued up
to flight altitude with the decay model, which is the truth a perfect sensor would read. The
vehicle: a scripted flight over it, and a magnetometer that reads the map plus everything the
vehicle does to the field: the rigid-body part (permanent, induced and eddy terms, the 18-term
Tolles-Lawson model of Problem 1, constant for a given vehicle) and the part that model cannot
describe (motor and ESC currents through an unknown geometry, battery current loops, servo
actuation, the second-order term when the platform field is large, eddy currents with a time
constant, temperature drift), all varying second by second. The estimator chain, which is the
demonstration: Tolles-Lawson calibration per vehicle removes the rigid-body part; a neural
corrector trained across thousands of randomised vehicles removes the rest using only telemetry
the vehicle already logs (attitude and rates, per-motor current or RPM, battery voltage and
current, throttle) together with the last ten to twenty seconds of the residual, inferring the
vehicle from that context rather than being told which one it is; a particle filter matches the
cleaned readings against a map reconstructed by the repository's network from a quarter of the
data, and reports position error in metres. The demo shows three traces (raw, after
Tolles-Lawson, after the corrector) and the metres each gives the navigator, over a 3D view of
the real map with the vehicle on it.

## The interference model (magsim/platform.py), invented for the demonstration and labelled so

Per vehicle, drawn at random from ranges so the corrector can be trained platform-agnostic:
- rigid body: the 18 Tolles-Lawson coefficients, magnitudes scaled by a platform class (survey
  fixed-wing about 460 nT, helicopter about 930 nT, multirotor about 2700 nT, as in
  problem1/edge_cases.py), plus the exact second-order magnitude term (the sensor reads the
  magnitude of the exact vector sum);
- eddy currents with a time constant tau drawn from 0.02 to 1 s (the lag model of edge_cases.py);
- motors: N motors (1 for fixed-wing, 1 for helicopter, 4 to 8 for multirotor), each a current
  loop at a fixed offset from the sensor with a random orientation; its field at the sensor is
  proportional to the motor current, which follows a throttle profile with PWM ripple at a
  random frequency; the telemetry reports each motor's current and RPM at 10 Hz;
- battery: a dipole proportional to total current at a fixed offset; telemetry reports voltage
  and current;
- servos: brief current pulses on control-surface actuation (fixed-wing), reported as a binary
  actuation flag;
- drift: a slow thermal offset, a random walk of a few nT per minute, not observable from
  telemetry (the corrector cannot remove it; the navigator has to absorb it as bias).
All of it is stated in magsim/README.md as invented physics with plausible magnitudes.

## The corrector (magsim/corrector.py, magsim/train_corrector.py)

Inputs at each step: attitude (3), rates (3), the direction cosines of the ambient field in the
body frame and their derivatives (the Tolles-Lawson regressors, 18), per-motor current and RPM
(padded to 8 motors), battery voltage and current, throttle, servo flag, and the linear-model
residual; the model sees a window of the last 100 to 200 steps (10 to 20 s at 10 Hz). Output:
the predicted residual at the current step. Architecture: a small 1-D convolutional or GRU
model, under 200k parameters. Training data: thousands of random vehicles, each flying several
manoeuvres, generated by the simulator; held-out vehicles for validation. Baselines, both from
edge_cases.py: Gauss-Newton on the exact rigid-body model, and the lag model. The table in
magsim/README.md reports residual rms after the linear model, after each baseline and after the
corrector, per platform class, on held-out vehicles, and separately for vehicles with and
without motor interference (the baselines cannot use telemetry; the corrector can; the table
should show exactly what that buys).

Read this whole file, then read README.md, docs/problem2.md (Parts E, F, G), problem1/README.md,
magscale/magnav.py, magscale/grid_eval.py, magscale/emag2.py, problem1/checks.py and
problem1/edge_cases.py before writing any code. Reuse those modules; do not reimplement the
Tolles-Lawson regressor, the upward continuation, the Wiener estimator, the network loading, or
the tile pipeline. The simulator is a demonstration of the results in this repository; every
panel must show a number that already appears in docs/ or results/, and the code that produces
it must be the code that produced the original.

Style: plain Python, comments that read as written by a person, no em dashes anywhere, no
emoji, no marketing language in docstrings or the README. Tests for every physical component.
Everything runs on a laptop CPU; the trained networks are loaded from runs/*.pt.

## Package layout

    magsim/
      __init__.py
      world.py        the map: a real grid (data/gawler_tmi.nc via magscale.grid_eval.load_grid)
                      or a synthetic one (magscale.gen2.sample_fields_v2), with the field at any
                      altitude by upward continuation and bilinear sampling of value and gradient
      aircraft.py     kinematics only: position (x, y, h), attitude (roll, pitch, yaw) and their
                      rates from scripted manoeuvres; no dynamics; a manoeuvre is a list of
                      (duration, roll_amp, pitch_amp, heading_rate) segments at 10 Hz
      platform.py     the platform field: the 18-term Tolles-Lawson model of problem1/checks.py
                      (permanent, induced, eddy) with the extensions of problem1/edge_cases.py
                      (second-order term for large fields, eddy currents with a time constant,
                      a switching current loop); presets: survey_fixed_wing, helicopter,
                      multirotor (parameters copied from edge_cases.py) and custom(seed, scale)
      sensor.py       scalar reading = |B_earth(x, y, h) + B_platform| + noise, using the exact
                      vector sum, so the first-order model is an approximation the calibration
                      has to live with (this is what makes the corrector necessary)
      calibrate.py    live Tolles-Lawson calibration: the regressor of problem1/checks.py built
                      row by row, least squares refit every second, the numerical rank of the
                      regressor so far, the residual rms; band-passed variant
      corrector.py    a small network (MLP or 1-D conv over the last two seconds of attitude,
                      rates, and the linear-model residual) that predicts the residual the
                      linear model leaves; training data comes from the simulator itself
                      (magsim/train_corrector.py); comparison baselines: Gauss-Newton on the
                      exact model and the lag model, both already in edge_cases.py
      navigator.py    particle filter over (x, y) on a given map: predict with the aircraft's
                      dead-reckoned motion plus noise, weight by the corrected scalar reading
                      against the map, resample; report the position error and the covariance
      maps.py         the maps to navigate on, all for the same ground: the true grid; the
                      grid reconstructed from 25% survey lines by the generator-trained network
                      (runs/vitxxl_gen2_hmix_D0_s0_72k), by the Gaussian estimator, and by
                      interpolation; reuse magscale.magnav's tiling and the line mask
      scenario.py     the scripted demo: calibration box on a fixed-wing, then the same on a
                      multirotor, then interference on with the corrector off and on, then a
                      survey leg with the navigator on each of the four maps
      viewer.py       Rerun logging: the map as a height-coloured mesh, the aircraft as a small
                      mesh or arrows with attitude, the flown path, the particle cloud, and
                      time series for: reading, platform field, calibration residual, rank,
                      corrector residual, position error, and the current tile's floor next
                      to the network's error
      run_demo.py     python -m magsim.run_demo --map gawler --scenario full --record demo.rrd
      README.md       what it simulates, what it does not (kinematics not dynamics; draped
                      survey continuation is approximate; platform models are invented for the
                      demo and calibrated against nothing real), how to run it, and which
                      number in docs/ each panel corresponds to
    tests/test_magsim.py

## Physics that must be right

- The scalar reading is the magnitude of the exact vector sum, not the first-order model.
- The Tolles-Lawson regressor is imported from problem1/checks.py; the rank readout must show
  17 raw and 16 band-passed for the box manoeuvre and 1 / 0 for a straight leg, as in the
  numerical checks. Add a test asserting this inside the simulator.
- Upward continuation uses magscale.grid_eval.continue_upward with reflect padding.
- The navigator's likelihood uses the map gradient for the Cramer-Rao readout, so the panel
  can show the bound next to the particle filter's actual error (Problem 1C).
- The maps in maps.py are produced by the same code path as the Part G evaluation; the
  reconstruction errors it reports must match results/grid_gawler.json to within seed noise.

## The corrector

Training data: run the simulator over all presets and a hundred random custom platforms, with
the extensions on, for a range of manoeuvres; record (attitude, rates, linear-model residual
history) and the true residual. Model: small, under 200k parameters. Train on Cuillin or the
Mac in under an hour; save to runs/corrector.pt with a json of config and validation numbers.
Report, in magsim/README.md: residual rms after the linear model, after Gauss-Newton, after the
lag model, after the corrector, for each platform preset, on held-out manoeuvres. The point is
that the corrector approaches the physics baselines without being told the physics; if it does
not, report that.

## The scripted demo (scenario full), about four minutes of simulated flight

1. Fixed-wing, calibration box: rank climbs to 17, residual falls below 1 nT. Text overlay
   quotes the corresponding line of problem1/checks.py.
2. Multirotor (2700 nT platform field): the same box; the linear model leaves tens of nT
   (edge_cases.py, three platform classes). Corrector on: residual drops; Gauss-Newton and lag
   baselines shown as two more traces.
3. Survey leg over the Gawler map at 60 m: navigator on the true map, then the network's map,
   the Gaussian estimator's map, interpolation. Position error readouts in metres; the
   particle cloud shown as a ridge after the first reading and a spot after the first turn.
4. Floor panel throughout the survey leg: current tile's Gaussian floor and the network's
   error on it, updating as the aircraft crosses tile boundaries.

## Non-goals

No dynamics, no wind, no attitude estimation from IMU (attitude is known to the simulator), no
real-time constraint (the demo plays a recording or runs faster than real time), no GPU at
demo time.
